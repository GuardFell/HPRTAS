# Socio-Technical (i\*) Models

The i\* models of the system, in both notations: **Strategic Dependency** (SD), which shows who
depends on whom for what, and **Strategic Rationale** (SR), which shows why each actor behaves the
way it does and what it is trying to achieve.

The models cover the actors in the pathway together with their hard goals, their soft goals, the
tasks they carry out, the resources they depend on and the dependencies between them. Because the
point of these models is the reasoning rather than the notation, the modelling choices and the
assumptions they rest on are recorded alongside them.

The SD and SR models describe the same situation from two angles, so they are checked against each
other: every dependency in the SD model has a rationale in the SR model, and every actor that
appears in one appears in the other with the same goals. Where they differ, the difference is
explained rather than left standing.

These models are analysis, not implementation. They are not deployed and no worker is bound to
them. What they justify is the shape of the operational processes in `../operational/` — which
responsibilities sit with which role, and which goals the process is arranged to protect.
